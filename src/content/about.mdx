I am a mathematician and AI scientist in Berlin. What drew me into mathematics, and keeps me there, is the geometry and topology of spaces one cannot see directly: structures that can still be understood, and very often drawn, with the right ideas.

I studied computer science, mathematics and physics together at Humboldt University of Berlin, in an interdisciplinary programme that runs all three subjects side by side, and specialised in differential geometry and topology. My bachelor's thesis, written under Chris Wendl, is about Seiberg–Witten theory and the differential topology of 4-manifolds ([PDF](/personal-website/thesis.pdf)): how equations from physics became one of the sharpest tools for telling four-dimensional spaces apart. I am now doing a master's in mathematics there.

My master's research brings that geometry to machine learning. I am writing my thesis with Frank Noé, in his AI4Science group at Freie Universität Berlin. Molecules come with geometry: their energy does not change when they are moved or turned, some of their motions are far stiffer than others, and realistic configurations concentrate on lower-dimensional structure. Flat, Euclidean coordinates ignore all of this and measure every direction of motion with the same ruler, as if stretching a bond were no different from twisting around it. My thesis is about machine learning that works in the geometry of molecules instead. The [article on shape space](/personal-website/blog/shape-space) grew out of the animations I made to think about it. I have also contributed to TopoBench, a library for topological deep learning.

Alongside the research I work in industry: I am an AI scientist in SAP's Business Foundation Model team, working on RPT, SAP's foundation model for tabular business data. Before that I spent four years building software. At SAP I worked on AI tooling and process automation, most recently the client of a platform that turns enterprise REST APIs into tools AI assistants can use. Earlier I built the optimisation core of an AI-assisted manufacturing system for rocket tanks at KPMG, worked on banking software at KfW, and wrote route optimisation software as a freelancer, together with a [visualiser of its algorithms](https://grapentt.github.io/RouteOptimizationVisualizer/).

## This site

Mathematics has an image problem. Physics gets films and documentaries; mathematics, in the public eye, is either a tool or a chore. That is a pity, because mathematics is the study of structure itself, far beyond the physical world, and it is beautiful. Part of the reason it stays hidden is that mathematicians write in a language that is precise and expressive but very hard to learn, and rarely translate back.

The blog is my attempt at translating. Every article is built around visualisations I make myself, and the newer ones are **interactive films**: narrated animations you can scrub through chapter by chapter, with scenes you can take over and explore. I aim for geometric intuition first and formulas second, without saying anything false along the way. If you find a mistake, or a passage that could be clearer, I would be glad to hear from you.
